Background commands started by the built-in agent (`bash(run_in_background)`) now run under a Rust-owned job supervisor instead of living in the chat sidecar's memory. Killing a background shell now reaps the whole process group, so `sh -c "pnpm dev"` no longer leaves vite/webpack alive holding the port after `kill_shell` reports success. Output is kept in a chunked in-memory ring backed by an on-disk log rather than a 256 KB buffer that was fully re-copied on every chunk, so a chatty build no longer burns CPU on memcpy and its earlier output stays readable — including after an app restart. `bash_output` gains a `from_offset` parameter for looking back through history (reads no longer consume), and combining `filter` with `wait_ms` now genuinely waits until a line matches instead of swallowing the non-matching output it skipped. `bash` gains an opt-in `detach` flag for work that must outlive the chat session; detached jobs are still reaped when the app exits, never left as orphan daemons. `start_process` with `detached: true` no longer produces an unsupervised orphan that discards its output and outlives the app — it now starts a tracked job whose output is readable and whose lifetime ends with the app, and `terminate_process` kills such a job's whole process group rather than signalling a single pid. Running background jobs now appear in the performance panel's Managed Processes view with their owning session or scheduled task, and can be stopped from there.

The built-in agent also gains `Monitor`, `monitor_list`, and `monitor_cancel`. A monitor can wait for a background job to exit, match a job-output pattern, poll a shell predicate with governed backoff, or wait for an upstream subagent/scheduled task. Short waits answer inline; long waits become durable watches that survive host restarts instead of consuming repeated model turns.
